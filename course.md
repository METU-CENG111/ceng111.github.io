## Catalog description

Introduction to the fundamentals of computer systems, including computer organization, operating systems, language processors and user interfaces. Introduction to algorithms and programming. Reasoning informally about the correctness and efficiency of programs. A functional programming language will be used for practical work.

**Credits:** 3+2

## Objective

To provide a basic understanding of fundamental concepts in computer science and engineering, and to improve the skills to work with abstract notions for solving computational problems. Teaching a particular programming language is not a primary objective; the language will serve as a medium for experimentation.

## Course conduct

Dear students,

CENG 111 is an introductory course for Computer Science concepts. In practice the course consists of two parts:

1. A first part in which an overview of current hardware (the von Neumann architecture) and its functionality is given. Following this, how basic data types are stored and handled is explained.
2. A second part where basic algorithmic and computational concepts are introduced following a functional paradigm. Python is used as the workbench.

The course conduct has four legs of support:

- Lectures
- Textbook(s)
- Labs
- Take-home exams

The following points are worth mentioning:

- The lectures are not a voicing of the text material. They cover aspects which are not covered in the course books, or put emphasis on some of the aspects.
- In the first part of the course the student is expected to gain a broad coverage of the introduced subjects mainly by him/herself. Internet search engines and encyclopedias are excellent sources for such broad-but-shallow information. As part of the university education strategy, students are expected to learn how to acquire knowledge directly by themselves. Therefore, if you are a student of the course, do trace the terminology which is unfamiliar to you. Make notes of it, and later during your self-study time consult the internet to get a grasp of the unfamiliar items.
- The laboratory study serves two purposes: to cover practical subjects which are not covered in the lectures (Linux setup, primary UNIX usage, the vi editor, conducting intelligent searches on the internet, and so on), and to experiment with Python and perform small algorithmic applications in a closed-lab environment.
- <!-- NOTE: the old site said "five Lab Exams" here but "Lab Exams (3-4)" in the grading table. Fix one of the two. -->There are several lab exams in total, from which you will gain an important fraction of your overall grade. The first lab exam contains practical Linux tasks; the remaining ones are Python programming tasks and are graded automatically by means of a testing program. Although the input/output testing of the lab exams is a "black-box test", your source code will be investigated for adherence to any constraints in the task specification.
- Take-home exams are assigned before each of the Python programming lab exams. These are for students to work on before the exams in order to practise their programming skills; they are not graded.
- Usually you will have 1–2 weeks to turn in your take-home exams. This time should be used properly. The level of the take-home exams is such that "hard work in the last 2–3 days" will not suffice. You are strongly advised to start concentrating on them right after you receive them.
